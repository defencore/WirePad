# Ready-board expansion audit — 2026-11-07

Owned scope: 21 new ready-made boards, 979 logical wiring contacts. The selected boards add Adafruit (9), Arduino (5), Waveshare (5) and NXP (2). Existing entries were checked before selection; this is a focused expansion, not an assertion that these manufacturers' entire ranges have been audited. Registration is owned by the root agent.

Source files: `catalog-ready-adafruit-more.js`, `catalog-ready-arduino-more.js`, `catalog-ready-waveshare-more.js`, `catalog-ready-nxp-more.js`, with `catalog-ready-more-tools.js` as the shared view builder. These files are the only repository files owned by this assignment.

All new canvas contacts use `surface:true` and `contactLayout:"physical"`. Contacts coincide with the pads drawn in the corresponding component-side, mirrored underside, or explicitly separated auxiliary connector face. Hidden faces have persistent captions, including when ordinary labels are disabled. Dense connectors retain terminal numbering and full contact functions in tooltips. Original catalog IDs/anchors are not edited by these files.

## Electrical and geometry checks

Adafruit and Arduino views/pad coordinates are derived from the exact published Eagle board files. Mirrored elements use the Eagle rotation-then-mirror transform; the displayed underside is separately mirrored into its own frame. Source copper contact centers, repeated castellated pads, drill sizes and terminal numbers feed the reference carrier. Manufacturer silk, board outlines, controls, connectors, antenna and major packages are embedded in each SVG; there are no external image dependencies. Adafruit source designs are attributed under the published CC BY-SA license.

Important verified distinctions: ItsyBitsy D5 is the unidirectional 5 V output and neither ItsyBitsy model has a LiPo charger; STM32F405 TP3 is the MCU RTC backup supply, distinct from the LiPo battery; ESP32 V2 input-only GPIOs retain input electrical types; RP2350 HSTX FFC retention/support pads are excluded from electrical terminals; QT Py repeated through-hole/castellated lands share terminal numbers. Unassigned prototype holes are drawn but are not invented numbered connectors. USB, microSD and RF ports are drawn as complete ports rather than general GPIO banks.

Arduino header, SWD, ESLOV, battery and documented test-pad numbers come from each board's own CAD/schematic. WAN1300/FOX battery power is the documented low-voltage two-cell supply, not a 3.7 V LiPo charging input. MKR WAN1310/GSM1400/NB1500 have their documented LiPo connection and ESLOV contacts. Unnamed `N$` test nets retain their original CAD identity without an assumed GPIO function. Placeholder WiFi1010 CAD URLs in shared docs YAML were not used for the five selected boards; the linked board-specific archives were downloaded and inspected.

Waveshare Zero generations have independently mapped underside contact order and pitch. RP2350 Plus pinout artwork is an underside view, so its front header order was verified after mirroring. ESP32-C6 Zero's schematic says FH4 while current docs say FH8; the entry explicitly describes fitted flash variants and uses the matching verified board contact map. Backside small-pad locations are reconstructed from manufacturer same-side photographs; these are not production connector land patterns. Relay NC contacts are passive normally-closed terminals, not unconnected pins. Relay input P17.2 is +5 V and P17.1 return. The carrier's optional Pico internal USB/BOOT assembly landings are outside the exposed wiring scope.

NXP includes all 64 main expansion contacts per board, target/OpenSDA debugging, documented supply/current links and selected user configuration pads. K64F additionally includes the RF header and optional Bluetooth UART. Debug headers have 1.27 mm pitch and their actual documented NC positions, including pin 9; other auxiliary link faces use their corresponding reference arrangement. E4 source checks corrected clock link to J8, reset selector to J25, added actual PHY route J14 and PHY supply J17, and verified the J18 supply order. KL25Z optional coin-cell contacts are functional `+`/`-` references, not asserted holder land-pattern numbers.

NXP auxiliary blocks are **separate connector/pad mating-face reference views**, identified persistently and matched to reference tags on the main board. Their actual PCB landing orientations/positions were not established by public source CAD and are not asserted. Board envelope and header placement are photo-derived reference geometry, with exact connector pitch/order. NXP internal manufacturing fixture/test pads, oscillator and USB shield links, and complete cable/card contacts are outside the explicitly described wiring scope. Manufacturer CAD archives are linked but may require an NXP account. No full-board production land-pattern or exhaustive factory-test-pad audit is claimed.

## QA artifacts and results

- 21 component definitions and 979 unique logical contacts; every contact is inside its component bounds and has a unique visible anchor within that board.
- Labeled/unlabeled self-contained SVGs and PNGs: `/tmp/wirepad-more/qa/` (42 of each); both modes visually reviewed, including narrow Zero/QT Py boards and persistent underside/auxiliary captions.
- `report.json` in that folder records per-entry sources, scope, logical contact and physical footprint-pad counts.
- Generated native KiCad symbols/footprints and native SVG renders: `/tmp/wirepad-more/qa-native/`. Symbol terminal-number sets and footprint pad-number sets are compared, preserving intentional repeated physical pads on one electrical terminal.
- `node --test`: all 115 tests pass. `git diff --check`: clean. New untracked source files are separately checked for whitespace diagnostics.
- Root owns final integrated editor image export, release/native integration checks, and comparison of the existing 444 baseline component anchors.

## Selection gaps

The proposed Pico ADC entry did not have a verified matching ready-board source and was not invented. Existing MKR WiFi1010, Arduino MKR Zero and other baseline models were not duplicated. Adalogger/Metro extra candidates were deprioritized to include both requested NXP FRDM boards in the fixed 21-entry assignment. Other Arduino Portenta/GIGA, newer Nordic DKs and complete remaining manufacturer lineups are not part of this focused selection.
